# Saturday

(The level's id stays `weekend-shift`; changing it would touch shared files. The player only ever sees the card.)

## Card

Friday, 4:00 PM. You're down to work Saturday.
The boss reads out the rota at 5:00. Keep your weekend.

## Name

"Saturday": somebody in this office has to come in and work on Saturday, and the board already says it's you. The name names the trouble (your weekend), not the way out. The old name, "Weekend Shift", read as the verb "shift", which gave away the trick. The card says it before you move, and the whiteboard in the middle of the room shows it: your face in the box under Saturday.

## The situation

You're an office worker, 4:00 on a Friday. The Saturday roster is a whiteboard on a stand in the middle of the office: a strip of seven day boxes along the top, Saturday's in red, and a red line from it down to one big box with a face in it. At 4:00 that face is yours. Nobody picks at 5:00: the board has already picked. At 5:00 the boss reads it out, and whoever's face is in the box works Saturday.

The boss filled the board in on Monday, along with a week of other rosters, and he doesn't remember whom he put down. He reads out whatever face is in the box. If you fail, you spend a sunny Saturday alone in the office at a desk with a pile of work. If you write Kev into the box, the boss reads out Kev's face instead, and Kev gets the Saturday.

The board is in full view. The boss, at his desk behind the glass, faces it, and so does Lin at her desk. You can't write on it while either is looking. You have to pull their eyes away from the board.

## Who does what

**The boss** sits at his desk behind the glass from 4:00 and keeps his eyes on the office. He watches for people, not for what's on the board: his sight cone lies on the board, so anyone who goes up to it and writes is in plain view. He glances away now and then, but never long enough to write a face. His desk has a folder on it and no phone. His phone stands on a little table in the top-right corner of his office, under the window. At 4:20 he goes to the copier and photocopies for five minutes with his back to the room (if it's jammed, he's cross: 😠). At 4:25 he's back at his desk. If his phone rings, he jumps (☎️❗), walks to the corner and answers it standing, facing the window, his back to the office. Nobody is on the line: he says hello (❓), waits, says it again (❓❓), and after about five seconds he slams it down (😠) and goes back to his desk. At 4:50 he comes out of his office for the reading with his head down in his folder (he thinks of the paperwork: 📄). He takes the black marker off the board's tray without looking up and sits on the chair beside the board with his back to it, facing the room. He's pleased with himself (😏): he guards the board, and from then on nobody writes on it under his nose. He hasn't looked at what it says since Monday. Just before 5:00 he announces it (📢), turns to the board, looks to see whom he put down (🧐), points the marker at the face in Saturday's box and says whose it is (👈). If Lin tells on you, he sends you to your desk, then walks out to the board, looks at it close up (🧐) and, if the face in the box isn't yours, wipes it and draws yours back (😠).

**Lin** sits at her desk in the bottom row, facing the board. Her lunch box sits on her desk: a fish in a plastic tub. At 4:30 she thinks of it (🐟), stands, pushes her chair out and carries the box to the kitchen. Her chair stays pushed out, turned away from her desk, the whole time she's gone. From about 4:32 to 4:37 she waits a step back from the microwave, facing it, her back to the room, while her fish turns in the lit window. At 4:37 she carries the fish back and eats it at her desk out of the open box (🐟😊). If she sees you write while the boss is busy, she points at you (❗👉) and tells him.

**Kev** sits at the far desk in the bottom row, too far off to see the board, his head down in his code. He's the one who fixes the machines. When the copier jams, he turns to it (❗), walks over and kneels at its open front with a spanner (🔧) for about four game minutes, until it stops beeping (😊). When Lin eats her fish two desks away, he looks over (🤢). If the boss reads out his face at 5:00, he protests (🙅); the boss points at the board (👈🗓️): the board says Kev.

**At 4:55** everyone gathers at the board for the reading: the boss on his chair beside it, and Lin and Kev on chairs facing it, with an empty chair between them. That chair is yours. If you aren't round the board when they sit down, each of them looks at your empty chair (❓).

## Things in the office

- **The Saturday board** (mid-room, west of centre). It's a whiteboard on a stand: seven day boxes, Saturday's red, and a red line from Saturday down to a big box with a face in it (yours at 4:00). A black marker and a red one lie in its tray. It's the level's whole problem, and the thing you write on. The black marker leaves the tray while you write, and the boss takes it at 4:50. In a real office: the boss writes the weekend rota on Monday and only reads it out on Friday.
- **The noodle cup** (the kitchen counter on the east wall, above the microwave). It's a cup of instant noodles with its lid on: your afternoon snack, and the reason you'd use the microwave. Nobody but you knows it's yours. When you start the microwave, the cup goes inside. After the ding it's back on the counter, lid open and steaming, so it doesn't block the microwave for Lin's fish.
- **The microwave** (the east wall, between the counter and the sink). It has a door window with a turntable inside and a panel with green digits and buttons. While it cooks, the window is lit and the cup turns in it, steaming, and the digits count down from 10. When it dings, a ripple spreads from it. Lin heats her fish in it at 4:30.
- **The sink and the fridge** (below the microwave). They're there to make the kitchen a kitchen. You can't use them.
- **The copier** (below-left of the board). It's an office copier with a paper tray and a status light. The boss photocopies at it at 4:20. You can jam it with a paper clip: a crumpled sheet sticks out, the light flashes red, and it beeps until Kev fixes it with a spanner. It's the trap: as the boss sees it from his desk, it's behind the board.
- **The supply room's phone** (top left, on a little table behind a solid wall). It's an internal phone that rings the boss's. The boss can't see you at it. When you ring, the handset lifts off its base, and a dotted line runs from it to his phone for as long as the call lasts.
- **The boss's phone** (the top-right corner of his office, on a side table under the window). It's his: his desk has none. He takes calls standing at it, his back to the office, the handset on a coiled cord.
- **Lin's lunch box** (on her desk). It's a fish in a plastic tub. It tells you at 4:00 that she'll go and heat it, and you see it go with her at 4:30.
- **The desks, chairs and plants.** The three desks in the bottom row are yours, Lin's and Kev's. The chairs round the board are for the reading: the boss's beside the board, and Lin's, yours and Kev's facing it. The plants make the office an office.

## Ways to win

Both routes end the same way. Write Kev's face into Saturday's box while nobody is looking, then be round the board for the reading. Writing takes 2.5 seconds and you see it happen: the black marker lifts off the tray, and the new face is scribbled over yours line by line, from the top. The new face pops up big off the board for a beat in a soft white ring, then settles into the box. The board still lights up after that: write again and it changes to the boss's face, which loses (see Ways to lose).

Why a real, half-awake boss falls for the ending: he filled the board in on Monday, with a week of other rosters, and doesn't remember whom he put down. He has watched the board all afternoon for people going up to it, not for what it says. At 4:50 he comes out reading his folder, takes the marker without looking and sits with his back to the board. When he turns at 5:00, he looks (🧐) and reads out the face that's there. Kev's face is a face he could have put down. Kev protests (🙅), and the boss points at the board (👈🗓️): the board says Kev, and he wrote the board.

In a real office: a manager reads out the rota he wrote days ago, and when someone says "I wasn't on it", he points at the rota.

### The ding

Steps:

1. Heat your noodles in the microwave.
2. Walk to the board before the ding.
3. When it dings, write.
4. Walk off, and be round the board when everyone sits down at 4:55. (Standing about idle where the boss can see you gets you sent to your desk, so pretend to work.)

Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: a microwave ding in a quiet office makes everyone look up at it, and everyone thinks of food. Then nobody comes for the noodles. Heads stay turned toward the kitchen for about six seconds, wondering whose they are (🍜❓). That's long enough for two and a half seconds of scribbling behind their backs.

In a real office: when a microwave dings and nobody comes for the food, people look over and wonder whose it is.

Facts, and where you see each one:

- **The board decides, and you're on it.** 4:00, the opening frame: the board mid-room shows your face in the red box under the red Saturday box. The card says your face is on it.
- **You can write on it.** Any time: walk up to the board and it lights up, with the E key cap on its far side. Two markers lie in its tray.
- **The boss and Lin are watching it.** From 4:00, their sight cones lie on the board. The boss's is drawn strongest. Lin's comes up from her desk.
- **You can heat noodles in the microwave.** Any time: a cup of instant noodles stands on the kitchen counter beside the microwave (east wall). Walk up and the microwave lights up. Press it and the cup goes into the lit window and turns there, steaming, and green digits count down from 10.
- **The ding turns every head toward the kitchen.** 10 seconds after you start it, wherever you are, the digits read 00 and a ripple spreads from the microwave. A 🍜❓ comes up over every head at once (whose noodles?), and every head and sight cone snaps east toward the kitchen. The boss's cone lies on the board right up to the ding, so you see it swing off. They stay turned for about 6 seconds. Then the noodles are back on the counter, open and steaming. Most players learn this the first time they heat their noodles, so the trick is one restart away.
- **The board is on the far side of the room from the microwave.** You see it in the opening frame: the board is on the west side, the microwave on the east wall. When the heads turn to the microwave, the board is behind them.
- **You must be at the board at 5:00.** At 4:55 everyone sits down round the board, leaving the chair between Lin and Kev empty. If you're elsewhere, they look at it (❓). The clock goes red at 4:50 and starts to tick.

### The phone

Steps:

1. Wait until about 4:30.
2. Go into the supply room (top left, behind a wall) and ring the boss's phone from the phone on its little table.
3. Walk to the board.
4. Write while the boss is on the phone in his corner and Lin is at the microwave.
5. Walk off, and be round the board when everyone sits down at 4:55.

Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: anybody gets up and answers their own ringing phone. Answering a silent line, anybody says "Hello? ... Hello?" before they hang up. The walk there and back and the two hellos take a few seconds, and he spends them facing the window. Nothing about the call needs him to stay on longer than that.

In a real office: a phone rings, you answer it, nobody's there, you say hello twice and hang up.

Facts, and where you see each one:

- **There's a phone that rings the boss's, out of his sight.** 4:00: a phone on a little table in the supply room, and one on a little table in the top-right corner of the boss's office. His desk has none, so that one is his. The supply room is behind a solid wall, so the boss can't see you at its phone. It's far from your seat (nothing is lit on the first frame): walk in and it lights up. When you ring, your handset lifts off its base, a dotted line runs from your phone to his, his phone shakes, and the boss jumps (☎️❗).
- **The boss takes the call standing at that phone, with his back to the office, for a few seconds.** Right after the ring, he walks to the corner and stands beside the table, facing the window. His handset is at his ear on a coiled cord from the base, so it's plainly that phone. Nobody's on the line: he says hello (❓), and again (❓❓), and he slams it down (😠), about five seconds after he picks up. Your handset stays off its base and the dotted line stays up for the whole call: it's your call. The ring, his walk to the corner and the call together give you time to walk from the supply room and write.
- **Lin still watches the board from her desk.** Her cone still lies on the board while the boss is on the phone. Write then and she points at you, and the boss sends you back to your desk to type for 20 minutes. You see the papers fly. Then he goes out to the board and checks it. Ringing straight away is the obvious first try, and that's how most players learn that Lin watches.
- **Lin leaves at 4:30 for the microwave.** At 4:30 she thinks of her fish (🐟), gets up with her lunch box and walks to the kitchen. Her desk is bare and her chair stays pushed out and turned away while she's gone. From about 4:32 to 4:37 she waits at the microwave with her back to the room while her fish turns in it. At 4:37 she carries it back to her desk.
- **So ring at about 4:30.** Ring while Lin is at the microwave, and both of them face away from the board for long enough for you to walk from the supply room to the board and write.

### The trap (not a win)

The copier, below-left of the board. Jam a paper clip in it: a crumpled sheet sticks out of its top, its light flashes red, and it beeps every 2 seconds until Kev has fixed it, with red arcs coming off it on every beep. Every head turns toward it. The boss thinks of it (🖨️), and an orange dashed line runs from his head to the copier. But as the boss sees it from his desk, the copier is lined up behind the board: his line to the copier runs straight through the board, and you at it are in plain view. A distraction pulls eyes toward something. This one pulls them through the board.

Why a real boss would catch you: a beeping copier is right behind the board from where he sits. Looking at it, he looks at you.

In a real office: you look at a noise, and you see whatever is between you and it.

## Ways to lose

- **Fail: your face is still on the board at 5:00.** On the map: just before 5:00, the boss announces it to the room (📢), turns to the board and points the marker at it (👈 your face), and the Saturday box glows round your face. He keeps it there until 5:00, then waves you up front (👈). End card: Saturday, the sun in the window, the office empty except for you at a desk with a pile of work. Your face is on the board beside you.
- **Caught: you wrote while the boss was looking.** On the map: the boss stops you (❗😠) and the screen shakes. The face you were halfway through scribbling stays on the board, with the marker at it. End card: you at the desk on Saturday, and the boss standing by the board, angry, pointing at you. The board still shows the half-drawn face over yours.
- **His own face: you wrote twice.** He never put himself down, and he knows it. On the map: just before 5:00 he turns to the board, looks (🧐), and finds his own face in the box (😳), red to the ears, hands spread. At 5:00 he wipes the slot, draws your face back in (✏️) and glares at you (😠👉 you). End card: you at the desk on Saturday, your face on the board, the boss beside it, angry, pointing at you. In a real office: nobody believes he put himself on the weekend rota, least of all him. (Away from the board at 5:00 as well: blamed.)
- **Told on: Lin saw you while the boss was away.** She points at you (❗👉) and tells the boss. He sends you back to your desk to type for 20 game minutes, papers flying, and the write she saw doesn't happen. Then he walks out to the board and looks at it (🧐). If an earlier write put Kev there, he wipes it and draws your face back (😠). This isn't an ending. If there's time, you can try again. In a real office: told someone was at the rota, a manager goes and checks the rota.
- **Blamed: you changed the board but weren't at it at 5:00.** Whoever isn't there gets Saturday. On the map: at 5:00 the boss sees your empty chair, turns to the board, wipes the slot and draws your face back in (✏️), then points at your chair (👉 you). Lin and Kev look at it too. End card: Friday at 5:00, round the board. The board shows your face again, the boss points at your empty chair, outlined red on the floor, and Lin and Kev have turned to look at it. The board still decides. In a real office: whoever isn't at the meeting gets the job nobody wants.

## The joke

From 4:50 the boss sits with his back to the board, holding its marker, smug (😏), sure of what it says, though he hasn't looked since Monday. Just before 5:00 he announces it, turns, looks, and reads out Kev's face. Kev protests (🙅). The boss points at the board (👈🗓️): the board says Kev. Then the card: a sunny Saturday, the office empty, and Kev alone at a desk, crying, his face on the board beside him. The row reads: a pencil, his face, a crying face. You did that to poor Kev.

Get greedy and write the boss in, and he knows at once: he never put himself down. He goes red, wipes it, draws you back in and glares.
