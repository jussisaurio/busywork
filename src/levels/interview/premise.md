# The Interview

## Card
3:00 PM. Lin wants your job. Her phone interview is at 3:30.
She must miss it.

The badge under it: Lin's face with her briefcase (her interview) and a red ringing handset (the call). The card's
clock face shows 3:30.

## Name
"The Interview": at 3:30 head office phones Lin to interview her for your job. That call is the trouble. If she takes
it, the job is hers. The name says what is coming and when. It says nothing about the switchboard, the hold music or
the plug in the wall, which are the ways out.

## The situation
You work in an open-plan office. Lin, who sits below the boss's glass wall, wants your job, and head office
interviews her for it by phone at 3:30. The call comes in on the office's outside line and rings her desk phone. If
she answers it at her desk, she gets your job. The end card shows her on the phone at your desk with your nameplate
and your red mug, her own desk empty behind her, while you stand by holding your things in a box.

The call has to fail. It fails when somebody else answers it, when her line is busy, when her phone is dead, or when
nobody answers it at all. Whatever happens, be back at your desk by the end of the call (3:34). From 3:30 the boss
keeps glancing at your chair. An empty chair while your job is being decided looks bad, even if the call failed. The
day is a time loop: when you lose, it is 3:00 again.

## Who does what
**The boss** (navy suit) sits in his high-backed oxblood chair in the glass office, top left, all afternoon. He faces
the open plan through the glass, so his sight cone covers Lin's desk and the wall socket beside it. He has a soft
spot for hold music. If his own phone rings, he gets up, takes it standing at his bright window with his back to the
room, and his empty chair turns to the window with him. Whatever phone he picks up, the tune keeps him for 9 minutes,
eyes on the view or straight ahead. Then the notes stop, he smiles and hangs up. When Lin's phone rings and nobody
else takes it, he is the nearest one who hears it, and he comes out of his door to answer it. When he answers her
3:30 call himself, he asks after Lin (her face, ?), glances at the kitchen door, shrugs and hangs up. If her phone
rings while he is on hold, he holds up a hand at it (bell, hand: not now) and stays with his tune.

**Lin** (teal cardigan) is nervous. From 3:00 she sits at her desk below the glass wall and checks her line: about
3:01 she lifts the receiver, listens to the tone (a teal wavy line by her head), puts it back and looks up at the wall
clock. At 3:03 she goes for a tea at the kitchen table, where she sits from about 3:07. At 3:22 she walks back with
her notes held up in front of her, mouthing her lines (notes), and from about 3:26 she sits in the chair beside her
desk facing the open plan, rehearsing and glancing at the clock. At 3:30 her phone rings and she takes the call. She
keeps her own line free: if her phone rings before 3:30 while she is at it, she picks up, hears hold music and slams
the receiver down, angry. The kitchen's red wall phone is on the outside line, like her interview, so if it rings
while she is free and near it, she answers it in hope (briefcase, ?) and stays on hold for 10 minutes. On the boss's
or Kev's phone she hangs up at once (shrug).

**Kev** (olive hoodie) has his coffee at the kitchen machine at 3:00. His own wall socket, by his desk on the right,
is broken: scorched, taped off with a yellow-and-black cross, and it sparks. So at 3:04 he ambles across the office
with his mobile held out (a red low-battery sliver) to the double socket by Lin's desk, about 3:10. He crouches,
plugs his charger into the bottom outlet under her phone's plug, and stands there scrolling while it charges, until
3:15. If her plug lies on the floor, it is in his way: !, he picks it up, holds it up at her empty chair, cross, and
jams it back into the top outlet with a spark. At 3:15 he takes his mobile back to the coffee machine (about 3:21)
and at 3:27 goes back to his desk. On any phone he holds for 4 minutes, then shrugs and hangs up.

**You** (red jumper) sit at the middle desk with the red mug.
- **The switchboard.** The office's old cord switchboard stands in the empty reception nook above your desk. Each
  jack has a card over it with a band and a lamp in its line's colour, and patch cables hang below. One card shows a
  briefcase: the outside line, which you cannot use. Its dark red lamp is there from the start. The others show Lin's
  face (teal), the boss's face (blue), Kev's face (olive) and a small red wall phone (the kitchen's, on the outside
  line). Plug into a jack and that phone rings: its lamp lights, the phone shakes, its handset hops, and a burst and
  rings in its line's colour spread from it to its earshot. Heads in earshot turn to it. The owner comes to answer if
  he is free and hears it, hurrying (!). If not, the nearest free person who hears it answers. A ring that nobody
  hears stops after a minute and a half. Whoever answers gets hold music (white notes out of the receiver) and stays
  on until he gives up (the times above). The lamp stays lit while the line is in use.
- **The plug.** You can crouch at the socket by Lin's desk and pull her phone's plug out of the wall (it takes a
  moment). The plug then lies on the floor, the top outlet shows two dark holes, her lamp on the switchboard goes
  black, and her phone is dead. You cannot do this while her phone is ringing or in use.
- Both are sneaky. If the boss sees you at either, you are caught. If Lin or Kev sees you, they shout and point, and
  the boss sends you to your desk with 20 minutes of work. Away from your desk the boss also grows suspicious if he
  sees you standing about. Pretending to be busy (shift) keeps him off you.

## Things in the office
- **The switchboard** (the nook above your desk): your tool. It is why one person can make phones ring all over the
  office. It is an old cord board left over from when the nook was reception, which is why nobody minds it.
- **The face cards and lamps** on it: which jack rings whose phone, and which lines are in use. The briefcase card
  and its red lamp are the outside line: where the interview will come from, and the colour of its ring.
- **Lin's teal desk phone, its coiled black cable and the double wall socket** beside her desk: the call rings here.
  The cable and the plug in the top outlet are on screen from 3:00, so pulling it is something the player can think
  of.
- **Kev's broken socket** (taped off, sparking, by his desk): why Kev charges at Lin's socket. It is the motive for
  his visit, shown before he makes it.
- **Kev's mobile** with its red battery sliver: why he goes to a socket at all. It turns green while it charges.
- **The boss's window and high-backed chair**: where he faces when he takes his own phone. The chair turning to the
  window says "his back is to the room" at a glance.
- **The glass wall** of the boss's office: he sees Lin's desk and the socket through it.
- **The kitchen's red wall phone**: the outside line's colour. It is why Lin answers it hoping it is her interviewer.
- **The kitchen table, the coffee machine, Lin's tea and Kev's coffee**: where Lin and Kev are when they are away,
  and why.
- **Lin's notes**: she carries them back at 3:22 and rehearses from them. They say "the call is close, she is
  getting ready" without words.
- **The wall clock**: Lin checks it. It says she is waiting for something with a time.
- **Your desk** with its nameplate and big red mug: the job at stake. Both turn up at Lin's elbows on the losing end
  card.
- **Lin's desk** (plant, teal mug, nameplate) and **Kev's desk** (code on the screen, olive mug): who sits where.
- **The paintings, plants, fridge and the kitchen window**: set dressing. They lead nowhere.

## Ways to win
You win when the 3:30 call fails and you are at your desk at 3:34. Each route has its own end card and route icon.

### 1. Someone else answers (route icon: shrug)
Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: it never touches him until 3:30. A phone ringing at an empty desk, and he
picks it up because somebody has to, asks for Lin, finds she is not in the kitchen doorway, and gives up.

Steps:
1. Between about 3:14 and 3:22 (not about 3:16, when Kev walks past the switchboard), while Lin is at her tea and
   Kev near the coffee machine, ring Kev's phone. Kev hears it and goes to his desk to answer it.
2. At once ring the kitchen phone. Kev is busy, so Lin, the free person nearest it, gets up from her tea and answers
   it. She holds for 10 minutes, past 3:30.
3. Go back to your desk.

At 3:30 Lin's desk phone rings red. Lin is still on hold in the kitchen. The boss starts (!), walks out to her phone
and answers it. He asks after Lin (her face, ?), glances at the kitchen door, shrugs and hangs up. Lin has missed her
call. The end card: the boss at her desk with her handset, puzzled, and Lin back from the kitchen, too late and
cross.

The facts, and when you see them:
- **Lin waits for a call at her desk phone.** At about 3:01 she listens on her receiver and looks up at the clock
  (shots `start`, `lin-checks-the-clock`).
- **The call comes at 3:30 on the outside line and rings her desk phone.** The card gives the time. On the first loop
  at 3:30 you see the briefcase jack's red lamp light, her phone ring red and Lin answer it, delighted (shot
  `the-call`).
- **The switchboard rings phones.** Up close it lights up: a row of jacks under face cards (shot `switchboard`). Plug
  into one and that phone rings in its line's colour, its lamp lit (shot `kev-phone-rings`).
- **The owner walks over, answers and stays on.** Kev hears his phone at the coffee machine (!), walks to his desk
  and stands there with the receiver and the hold music (shots `kev-phone-rings`, `kev-on-hold`).
- **A ring goes to the nearest free person who hears it.** With Kev on his way to his own phone, Lin answers the
  kitchen phone (shot `lin-takes-the-kitchen-phone`). Ring the kitchen alone after about 3:20 and Kev, nearer, takes
  it, so she is free again at 3:30 (the teaching fail).

A variant: Kev out of the way by timing instead of a phone call. Between about 3:14 and 3:19 Kev is at Lin's socket
or walking back from it, out of earshot of the kitchen. Ring the kitchen phone alone then (not at about 3:16, when
Kev walks past the switchboard), and Lin, alone in the kitchen with her tea, answers it and stays on hold past 3:30.
The rest is route 1. Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: the same as route 1, and Lin's part is just as real:
she is alone in the kitchen, the outside-line phone rings, and she picks it up hoping it is her interviewer. It
teaches the same lesson as route 1 (the ring goes to whoever is free and near), learned from Kev's day instead of
from his phone.
- **Lin holds on, hoping it is her interviewer.** The kitchen phone is red, the outside line's colour. On it she says
  briefcase, ? and stays on. She is still there at 3:30 (shot `the-call-answered`).
- **The boss takes Lin's phone when she is away.** At 3:30 he starts and gets up to answer it (shot
  `the-call-answered`).

### 2. Her line is busy (route icon: notes)
Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: a phone rings at an empty desk, he answers it, gets put on hold and likes
the music. Nobody hangs up on a hold queue in the middle of a nice tune, and nobody asks him to.

Steps:
1. Between about 3:18 and 3:25, while Lin is away from her desk, ring Lin's phone. Not at about 3:23, when she walks
   past the switchboard on her way back: she would see you and tell on you.
2. Go back to your desk.

The boss hears it (!), comes out of his office to her desk and answers it. He likes the hold music and stays on her
line for 9 minutes. Lin comes back at about 3:26 and finds him on her phone. At 3:30 her interviewer gets a busy
line. Lin turns to the boss: her handset, his face, angry.

The busy window is about 7 game minutes, about 11 seconds of real time. That is over the 5-second floor, so the hold
stays at 9 minutes.

The facts, and when you see them:
- **The boss answers Lin's phone when she is away, and stays on.** Shot `boss-on-lins-phone`: her teal phone rings
  on her empty desk and the boss comes out of his door to it. Shot `the-call-busy`: he is still on it at 3:30.
- **While Lin sits at her phone, she slams it down.** You see her pick up, hear the tune and slam the receiver down,
  angry (shot `lin-hangs-up-on-her-own-phone`).
- **Too early, and the boss is off her line again before 3:30.** He stays on for 9 minutes. Ring before about 3:18
  and you see him hang up and walk back to his office before the call comes.
- **A busy line means no call.** At 3:30 the boss is still on her line and Lin glares at him (shot
  `the-call-busy`).

### 3. Her phone is dead (route icon: plug)
Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: he is on the phone at his window with his back to the room, so he does not
see you crouch at a socket. Plugs get knocked out all the time, and nobody crawls under a desk to check one. Lin
waits for a late call; she does not think to check the wall.

Steps:
1. From about 3:14, as Kev's charging stop ends, ring the boss's phone. He stands at his window with his back to the
   room for 9 minutes.
2. While he is on hold, between about 3:17 and 3:24 (Kev gone back to the kitchen, Lin still at her tea), go to the
   socket by Lin's desk and pull her plug out.
3. Go back to your desk.

At 3:30 nothing rings. Lin sits at her silent phone and wonders why her interviewer is late (clock, ?).

The facts, and when you see them:
- **Her phone needs its plug.** From the start you see the coiled cable run from her phone to a socket in the wall
  beside her desk, its plug in (shot `start`). After you pull it, the plug lies on the floor, the top outlet shows
  two dark holes and her lamp on the switchboard is black (shot `cord-pulled`).
- **The boss watches the socket.** His sight cone goes through the glass over Lin's desk and the socket (shot
  `start`). If you pull the plug while he looks, he points at you (shot `end-caught`).
- **On his own phone the boss turns his back.** He stands at the window with the receiver, his chair turned to the
  window too, his sight cone pointing out of the window (shot `boss-on-hold`).
- **Kev charges his mobile at that socket from about 3:10 to 3:15, and why.** His own socket is taped off and sparks
  (shot `kev-at-the-socket`). He stands at Lin's socket scrolling, his charger in under her plug. If her plug is out,
  it lies in his way: !, he holds it up at her chair, cross, and jams it back in with a spark (shot
  `kev-plugs-it-back`).
- **A dead phone means no call.** At 3:30 Lin waits at her silent phone with its plug on the floor (shot
  `the-call-dead`).

### 4. Nobody answers (route icon: bell)
Why a real, half-awake boss falls for it: he is in the middle of a call of his own. A boss on the phone waves at
another phone to wait; he does not hang up his call to run for someone else's.

Steps:
1. At about 3:18 to 3:20 ring Kev's phone, then the kitchen phone, as in route 1. Lin is on hold in the kitchen past
   3:30.
2. Between about 3:22 and 3:29, ring the boss's phone. He is on hold at his window past 3:30.
3. Go back to your desk.

At 3:30 Lin's phone rings red at her empty desk. Lin is on hold in the kitchen and the boss at his window: he holds
up a hand at the ring (bell, hand) and stays with his tune. Kev is back at his desk across the office, too far away
to hear it. It rings for two minutes and stops. The end card: Lin's phone ringing in front of her empty chair, and at
the back the boss and Lin each on hold.

The facts, and when you see them:
- Everything in route 1, plus: **the boss is the one who answers Lin's phone at 3:30**, and he cannot while he is on
  his own phone (shots `the-call-answered`, `boss-on-hold`).
- **He is on the phone for 9 minutes.** Ring him before about 3:22 and he hangs up in time to answer it (route 1
  again). Ring him alone and Lin is back at her desk to take her call (the teaching fail).
- **Nobody comes.** Shot `the-call-rings-out`: the red rings at her empty desk, Lin on hold in the kitchen, the boss
  on hold at his window holding up a hand at the ring, Kev at his far desk.

## Ways to lose
- **Lin takes her call.** The end card: Lin at your desk with your nameplate and your red mug, on the phone, beaming
  (briefcase, smile); her own desk stands empty behind. You stand by holding your things in a box (shot `end-fail`).
- **Caught.** The boss sees you at the socket. The end card: you at the wall with Lin's plug in your fist and the
  boss pointing at you, angry (shot `end-caught`).
- **Away from your desk.** The call failed, but at 3:34 your chair is empty. The end card: the boss points at your
  empty chair and your red mug, while you hurry in late (shot `end-blamed`).
- **Told on.** Not a loss by itself. Lin or Kev sees you at the switchboard or the socket and points. The boss sends
  you to your desk with 20 minutes of work, and usually the window has closed when you are free.
- The teaching fails: each picture shows why, and each ends with Lin taking her call. Ring only the kitchen late and
  Kev answers it. Ring Lin's phone while she sits at it and she slams it down. Pull the plug before Kev's charging
  stop and he jams it back in. Ring only the boss and Lin is at her desk at 3:30.

## The joke
3:26 to 3:30 on the busy route. Lin hurries back from her tea, rehearsing from her notes for the call that will get
her your job, and finds the boss at her desk with her phone at his ear, eyes half shut, enjoying the hold music. He
does not budge. At 3:30 her interviewer gets a busy line, and Lin, beside him, glares at her own boss.
