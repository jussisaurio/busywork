# The Interview

## Card
3:00 PM. Lin wants your job. Head office interviews her by phone at 3:30.
They ring once. She must miss it.

The badge under it: Lin's face with her briefcase (her interview) and a red ringing handset (the call). The card's
clock face shows 3:30.

## Name
"The Interview": at 3:30 head office phones Lin to interview her for your job. That call is the trouble. If she takes
it, the job is hers. The name says what is coming and when. It says nothing about the switchboard, the calls waiting
on it or the plug in the wall, which are the ways out.

## The situation
You work in an open-plan office. Lin, who sits below the boss's glass wall, wants your job. Head office, not the
boss, interviews her for it by phone at 3:30. It is one call in a day of interviews, and they ring once: if nobody
picks up, or the line is busy or dead, they move on to the next name. The call comes in on the office's outside line
and rings her desk phone. If she answers it at her desk, she gets your job. The end card shows her on the phone at
your desk with your nameplate and your red mug, her own desk empty behind her, while you stand by holding your things
in a box.

The call has to fail. It fails when somebody else answers it, when her line is busy, when her phone is dead, or when
nobody answers it at all. Whatever happens, be back at your desk by the end of the call (3:34). From 3:30 the boss
keeps glancing at your chair. An empty chair while your job is being decided looks bad, even if the call failed. The
day is a time loop: when you lose, it is 3:00 again.

## Who does what
Two calls wait on hold at the old switchboard all afternoon. **Gary**, the boss's golf buddy, holds for the boss and
never stops talking. **Head office's queue** ("please hold for HR") holds on the outside line. Put a call through and
whoever answers gets that caller.

**The boss** (navy suit) sits in his high-backed oxblood chair in the glass office, top left, all afternoon. He faces
the open plan through the glass, so his sight cone covers Lin's desk and the wall socket beside it. On any inside
phone Gary is on, the boss is delighted (golf flag, grin) and they talk golf for 9 minutes; he laughs out loud every
minute or so. His own phone he takes standing at his bright window with his back to the room, and his empty chair
turns to the window with him. Then he smiles and hangs up. When Lin's phone rings and nobody else takes it, he is the
nearest one who hears it, and he comes out of his door to answer it. When he answers her 3:30 call himself, he tells
the caller she is not at her desk (her face, her empty chair), shrugs and hangs up. If her phone rings while he is on
with Gary, he holds up a hand at it (bell, hand: not now) and talks on.

**Lin** (teal cardigan) is nervous. From 3:00 she sits at her desk below the glass wall and checks her line: about
3:01 she lifts the receiver, listens to the tone (a teal wavy line by her head), puts it back and looks up at the wall
clock. At 3:03 she goes for a tea at the kitchen table, where she sits from about 3:07. At 3:22 she walks back with
her notes held up in front of her, mouthing her lines (notes), and from about 3:26 she sits in the chair beside her
desk facing the open plan, rehearsing and glancing at the clock. At 3:30 her phone rings and she takes the call.
One rule decides every call she answers: she stays on anything that could be her interview and gets off anything
that can't. Gary asks for the boss (golf flag, the boss's face). On her own phone she hangs up on him at once, cross,
to keep her line free; on the boss's or Kev's phone she just hangs up. The kitchen's red wall phone is on the outside
line, like her interview. On it she hears head office's "please hold for HR" (briefcase, hourglass): it might be her
interview, early. She crosses her fingers and holds for 10 minutes, past 3:30; nobody hangs up on HR four minutes
before an interview. If she finds the boss on her own phone, she hovers and glances at him (her handset, his face, a
grimace): she is too scared to cut in on the boss.

**Kev** (olive hoodie) has his coffee at the kitchen machine at 3:00. His own wall socket, by his desk on the right,
is broken: scorched, taped off with a yellow-and-black cross, and it sparks. So at 3:04 he ambles across the office
with his mobile held out (a red low-battery sliver) to the double socket by Lin's desk, about 3:10. He crouches,
plugs his charger into the bottom outlet under her phone's adapter, and stands there scrolling while it charges,
until 3:15. If her adapter lies on the floor, it is in his way: !, he picks it up, holds it up at her empty chair,
cross, and jams it back into the top outlet with a spark. At 3:15 he takes his mobile back to the coffee machine
(about 3:21) and at 3:27 goes back to his desk. Whatever phone he answers, he is stuck on it for 4 minutes: Gary
talks golf at him (golf flag, glum) and Kev is too polite to cut him off. Then he shrugs and hangs up.

**Who hears a phone.** A ring carries about 6 steps; the rings drawn round a ringing phone show how far. The boss's
glass office is right above Lin's desk, about 3 steps from her phone, so he hears it. The kitchen is across the
office, about 19 steps away, so from the kitchen Lin does not hear her desk phone, least of all with a receiver at her
ear. Kev's desk on the far right is out of its earshot too.

**You** (red jumper) sit at the middle desk with the red mug.
- **The switchboard.** The office's old cord switchboard stands in the empty reception nook above your desk. Each
  jack has a card over it with a band and a lamp in its line's colour, and patch cables hang below. One card shows a
  briefcase: the outside line itself, which you cannot use. Its dark red lamp is there from the start. The others
  show Lin's face (teal), the boss's face (blue), Kev's face (olive) and a small red wall phone (the kitchen's, on the
  outside line). Plug into a jack and the waiting call is put through: Gary to Lin's, the boss's or Kev's phone, head
  office's queue to the kitchen phone. The phone rings: its lamp lights, the phone shakes, its handset hops, and a
  burst and rings in its line's colour spread from it to its earshot. Heads in earshot turn to it. The owner comes to
  answer if he is free and hears it, hurrying (!). If not, the nearest free person who hears it answers. A ring that
  nobody hears stops after a minute and a half. Whoever answers gets the caller: Gary talking (white talk marks out of
  the receiver) or HR's hold music (white notes), and reacts as above. The lamp stays lit while the line is in use.
- **The plug.** Lin's desk phone runs off a power adapter in the double wall socket by her desk. You can crouch at the
  socket and pull the adapter out (it takes a moment). It then lies on the floor, the top outlet shows two dark
  holes, her lamp on the switchboard goes black, and her phone is dead. You cannot do this while her phone is ringing
  or in use.
- Working the switchboard is nothing to see, and nobody minds you at it, with one exception: Lin, if she watches you
  put a call through to her own line, which she is keeping free. Pulling the plug is sneaky, and anyone within arm's
  reach of you notices it, whatever way they face. If the boss sees you at the socket, you are caught. If Lin sees you
  put a call through to her line, or Lin or Kev sees you at the socket, they shout and point, and the boss sends you
  to your desk with 20 minutes of work. Away from your desk the boss also grows suspicious if he sees you standing
  about. Pretending to be busy (shift) keeps him off you.

## Things in the office
- **The switchboard** (the nook above your desk): your tool. It is an old cord board left over from when the nook was
  reception, and callers still wait on it for an extension. Putting a waiting call through is what a switchboard is
  for, which is why nobody minds you at it.
- **The face cards and lamps** on it: which jack rings whose phone, and which lines are in use. The briefcase card
  and its red lamp are the outside line: where the interview will come from, and the colour of its ring.
- **Lin's teal desk phone, its black power cable and adapter, and the double wall socket** beside her desk: the call
  rings here. The cable and the adapter in the top outlet are on screen from 3:00, so pulling it is something the
  player can think of.
- **Kev's broken socket** (taped off, sparking, by his desk): why Kev charges at Lin's socket. It is the motive for
  his visit, shown before he makes it.
- **Kev's mobile** with its red battery sliver: why he goes to a socket at all. It turns green while it charges.
- **The boss's window and high-backed chair**: where he faces when he takes his own phone. The chair turning to the
  window says "his back is to the room" at a glance.
- **The glass wall** of the boss's office: he sees Lin's desk and the socket through it.
- **The kitchen's red wall phone**: on the outside line, in its colour. Head office's HR queue comes through on it,
  which is why Lin holds on it, hoping it is her interview.
- **The kitchen table, the coffee machine, Lin's tea and Kev's coffee**: where Lin and Kev are when they are away,
  and why.
- **Lin's notes**: she carries them back at 3:22 and rehearses from them. They say "the call is close, she is
  getting ready" without words.
- **The wall clock**: Lin checks it. It says she is waiting for something with a time.
- **Your desk** with its nameplate and big red mug: the job at stake. Both turn up at Lin's elbows on the losing end
  card.
- **Lin's desk** (plant, teal mug, nameplate) and **Kev's desk** (code on the screen, olive mug): who sits where.
- **The paintings, plants, fridge and the kitchen window**: set dressing. They lead nowhere.

## Ways to win
You win when the 3:30 call fails and you are at your desk at 3:34. Each route has its own end card and route icon.

### 1. Someone else answers (route icon: shrug)
In a real office: nobody is fooled. Lin is on hold for HR in the kitchen, as anyone waiting on HR would be, out of
earshot of her desk. The boss answers a phone ringing at an empty desk because somebody has to, says she is not at
her desk and hangs up. Head office rings once and moves on.

Steps:
1. Between about 3:14 and 3:21, while Lin is at her tea and Kev near the coffee machine or at Lin's socket, ring
   Kev's phone. Kev hears it and goes to his desk to answer it (Gary talks golf at him).
2. At once ring the kitchen phone. Kev is busy, so Lin, the free person nearest it, gets up from her tea and answers
   it: "please hold for HR". She holds for 10 minutes, past 3:30.
3. Go back to your desk.

At 3:30 Lin's desk phone rings red. Lin is still on hold in the kitchen. The boss starts (!), walks out to her phone
and answers it. She is not at her desk (her face, her empty chair): he shrugs and hangs up. Lin has missed her call.
The end card: the boss at her desk with her handset, puzzled, and Lin back from the kitchen, too late and cross.

The facts, and when you see them:
- **Lin waits for a call at her desk phone.** At about 3:01 she listens on her receiver and looks up at the clock
  (shots `start`, `lin-checks-the-clock`).
- **The call comes at 3:30 on the outside line and rings her desk phone.** The card gives the time. On the first loop
  at 3:30 you see the briefcase jack's red lamp light, her phone ring red and Lin answer it, delighted (shot
  `the-call`).
- **The switchboard rings phones.** Up close it lights up: a row of jacks under face cards (shot `switchboard`). Plug
  into one and that phone rings in its line's colour, its lamp lit (shot `kev-phone-rings`).
- **The owner walks over, answers and is stuck on it.** Kev hears his phone at the coffee machine (!), walks to his
  desk and stands there with the receiver while Gary talks at him (shots `kev-phone-rings`, `kev-on-hold`).
- **A ring goes to the nearest free person who hears it.** With Kev on his way to his own phone, Lin answers the
  kitchen phone (shot `lin-takes-the-kitchen-phone`). Ring the kitchen alone after about 3:20 and Kev, nearer, takes
  it, so she is free again at 3:30 (the teaching fail).
- **Lin holds on for HR.** The kitchen phone is red, the outside line's colour. On it she hears head office's hold
  (briefcase, hourglass), crosses her fingers and stays on, hold music in her ear. She is still there at 3:30 (shot
  `the-call-answered`).
- **The boss takes Lin's phone when she is away.** At 3:30 he starts and gets up to answer it (shot
  `the-call-answered`).

A variant: Kev out of the way by timing instead of a phone call. Between about 3:14 and 3:19 Kev is at Lin's socket
or walking back from it, out of earshot of the kitchen. Ring the kitchen phone alone then, and Lin, alone in the
kitchen with her tea, answers it and holds for HR past 3:30. The rest is route 1. In a real office: the same as route
1; she is the only one in the kitchen when the outside-line phone rings. It teaches the same lesson as route 1 (the
ring goes to whoever is free and near), learned from Kev's day instead of from his phone.

### 2. Her line is busy (route icon: golf flag)
In a real office: a phone rings at an empty desk, the boss answers it, and it is his golf buddy, who won't stop
talking. A boss happily chats for 9 minutes. Lin comes back to find her boss laughing on her phone, and she is too
scared to interrupt him. At 3:30 head office gets a busy line and moves on.

Steps:
1. Between about 3:18 and 3:24, while Lin is away from her desk, ring Lin's phone. Not at about 3:23, when she walks
   past the switchboard on her way back: she would see you put a call through to her line and tell on you.
2. Go back to your desk.

The boss hears it (!), comes out of his office to her desk and answers it: Gary (golf flag, grin). They talk golf for
9 minutes, the boss laughing out loud. Lin comes back at about 3:26 and finds him on her phone. She hovers (her
handset, his face, a grimace). At 3:30 her interviewer gets a busy line. Lin turns to the boss: her handset, his
face, angry.

The busy window is about 7 game minutes, about 11 seconds of real time. That is over the 5-second floor, so the boss's
call stays at 9 minutes.

The facts, and when you see them:
- **The boss answers Lin's phone when she is away, and stays on.** Shot `boss-on-lins-phone`: her teal phone rings
  on her empty desk and the boss comes out of his door to it. Shot `the-call-busy`: he is still on it at 3:30.
- **Gary wants the boss, and Lin keeps her line free.** Ring her phone while she sits at it: she picks up, Gary asks
  for the boss (golf flag, the boss's face) and she slams the receiver down, cross (shot
  `lin-hangs-up-on-her-own-phone`). Only the boss stays on with Gary.
- **Too early, and the boss is off her line again before 3:30.** He stays on for 9 minutes. Ring before about 3:18
  and you see him hang up and walk back to his office before the call comes.
- **A busy line means no call.** At 3:30 the boss is still on her line and Lin glares at him (shot
  `the-call-busy`).

### 3. Her phone is dead (route icon: plug)
In a real office: the boss is laughing with Gary at his window, his back to the room, so he does not see you crouch
at a socket. Adapters get knocked out all the time, and nobody crawls under a desk to check one. Lin waits for a late
call; she does not think to check the wall.

Steps:
1. From about 3:14, as Kev's charging stop ends, ring the boss's phone. It is Gary: the boss stands at his window with
   his back to the room for 9 minutes.
2. While he is on with Gary, between about 3:17 and 3:24 (Kev gone back to the kitchen, Lin still at her tea), go to
   the socket by Lin's desk and pull her phone's adapter out. Nobody else may be within arm's reach of you there.
3. Go back to your desk.

At 3:30 nothing rings. Lin sits at her silent phone and wonders why her interviewer is late (clock, ?).

The facts, and when you see them:
- **Her phone needs its adapter.** From the start you see the cable run from her phone to the adapter in the socket
  beside her desk (shot `start`). After you pull it, the adapter lies on the floor, the top outlet shows two dark
  holes and her lamp on the switchboard is black (shot `cord-pulled`).
- **The boss watches the socket.** His sight cone goes through the glass over Lin's desk and the socket (shot
  `start`). If you pull the plug while he looks, he points at you (shot `end-caught`).
- **Anyone within arm's reach notices.** Crouch at the socket while Kev charges his mobile there, or as Lin comes back
  past it, and they turn to look, whatever way they faced, and tell on you.
- **On his own phone the boss turns his back.** He stands at the window with the receiver, his chair turned to the
  window too, his sight cone pointing out of the window (shot `boss-on-hold`).
- **Kev charges his mobile at that socket from about 3:10 to 3:15, and why.** His own socket is taped off and sparks
  (shot `kev-at-the-socket`). He stands at Lin's socket scrolling, his charger in under her adapter. If her adapter
  is out, it lies in his way: !, he holds it up at her chair, cross, and jams it back in with a spark (shot
  `kev-plugs-it-back`).
- **A dead phone means no call.** At 3:30 Lin waits at her silent phone with its adapter on the floor (shot
  `the-call-dead`).

### 4. Nobody answers (route icon: bell)
In a real office: the boss is mid-chat with his golf buddy. A boss on his own call waves at another phone to wait; he
does not hang up on a friend to run for someone else's phone. Lin is on hold for HR in the kitchen, out of earshot.

Steps:
1. At about 3:18 to 3:20 ring Kev's phone, then the kitchen phone, as in route 1. Lin holds for HR in the kitchen past
   3:30.
2. Between about 3:22 and 3:29, ring the boss's phone. He talks with Gary at his window past 3:30.
3. Go back to your desk.

At 3:30 Lin's phone rings red at her empty desk. Lin is on hold in the kitchen and the boss at his window: he holds
up a hand at the ring (bell, hand) and talks on. Kev is back at his desk across the office, too far away to hear it.
It rings for two minutes and stops. The end card: Lin's phone ringing in front of her empty chair, and at the back
the boss talking at his window and Lin on hold in the kitchen.

The facts, and when you see them:
- Everything in route 1, plus: **the boss is the one who answers Lin's phone at 3:30**, and he cannot while he is on
  his own phone (shots `the-call-answered`, `boss-on-hold`).
- **He is on the phone for 9 minutes.** Ring him before about 3:22 and he hangs up in time to answer it (route 1
  again). Ring him alone and Lin is back at her desk to take her call (the teaching fail).
- **Nobody comes.** Shot `the-call-rings-out`: the red rings at her empty desk, Lin on hold in the kitchen, the boss
  on the phone at his window holding up a hand at the ring, Kev at his far desk.

## Ways to lose
- **Lin takes her call.** The end card: Lin at your desk with your nameplate and your red mug, on the phone, beaming
  (briefcase, smile); her own desk stands empty behind. You stand by holding your things in a box (shot `end-fail`).
- **Caught.** The boss sees you at the socket. The end card: you at the wall with Lin's adapter in your fist and the
  boss pointing at you, angry (shot `end-caught`).
- **Away from your desk.** The call failed, but at 3:34 your chair is empty. The end card: the boss points at your
  empty chair and your red mug, while you hurry in late (shot `end-blamed`).
- **Told on.** Not a loss by itself. Lin sees you put a call through to her own line, or Lin or Kev sees you at the
  socket, and points. The boss sends you to your desk with 20 minutes of work, and usually the window has closed when
  you are free.
- The teaching fails: each picture shows why, and each ends with Lin taking her call. Ring only the kitchen late and
  Kev answers it. Ring Lin's phone while she sits at it and she hangs up on Gary. Pull the plug before Kev's charging
  stop and he jams it back in. Ring only the boss and Lin is at her desk at 3:30.

## The joke
3:26 to 3:30 on the busy route. Lin hurries back from her tea, rehearsing from her notes for the call that will get
her your job, and finds the boss at her desk with her phone at his ear, roaring at his golf buddy's stories. He does
not budge, and she does not dare ask. At 3:30 her interviewer gets a busy line, and Lin, beside him, glares at her
own boss.
