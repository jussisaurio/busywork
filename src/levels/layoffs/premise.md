# The Layoff List

## Card
"Friday, 3:00 PM. You're on the layoff list."
"It goes to HR at 3:45. Get off it."

## Name
The Layoff List is the thing on the boss's screen: a white sheet with a red title bar and a row of four faces, yours
framed in red. At about 3:08, when Kev gets the computer working, the sheet pops out large beside the screen, your
frame throbbing, and you shudder (😨). After that you can read it from your own desk, through the glass of his
office and over his shoulder. The name says what the danger is and where it is before the player has solved
anything.

## The situation
- You are the office worker in the red sweater at the middle desk of the open floor. You want to keep your job.
- At 3:45 the boss clicks send, and the list goes to HR. If your face is on it, you carry your desk out in a box.
- HR gets what is on his screen at 3:45. Take your face off the sheet before then and the list goes without you.
- His office is glass. He sits with his back to the glass and his big screen faces out, so the whole floor can
  read it over his shoulder. Lin's desk faces his office. Anyone in his chair is in her view, and so is his
  screen.
- No costumes. In this game a person is their face, and you are always you. Each step uses a fact about who
  looks where and when, and the screen shows that fact before you need it.

## Cast
- **The boss**: bald, navy suit, red tie, moustache.
  - 3:00: at his desk. His monitor is off, plain black, its power light dark. He bangs on it, and it stays black.
  - 3:01: he phones Kev's desk (☎️ and Kev's face). Kev's desk phone rings (rings spread from it). Lin turns
    round in her chair to the ringing. Kev picks up.
  - 3:04 to 3:08: he stands beside his desk and watches Kev kneel behind it.
  - About 3:08: the screen blinks on and the list comes up. He smiles and sits down.
  - 3:10: a restroom break. When he gets up, a padlock comes up on his screen over the list. He walks out across
    the floor. If you try his screen while he is away, the padlock jolts and flashes red (your thought: 🔒).
  - He walks back from the restroom and is at his desk about 3:22. At his desk the padlock goes away.
  - Left alone, the screen keeps working: he works on at his desk until 3:42 and never leaves again.
  - If you pulled his monitor's plug out while he was away (behind his desk, where Kev knelt), his monitor is black
    when he sits down, but the PC behind it is still on. He types his password into it anyway: five asterisks over
    his head and a clack for each (✱✱✱✱✱), then a hard Enter (⏎, a loud key). The PC logs him in, unseen. He
    stares at the black screen (thought: 🖥️❓) and thumps it (😠). He phones Kev's desk again (☎️ and Kev's
    face). The phone rings and rings at the empty desk. Then he has a thought with Kev's face and a question mark.
    Nobody comes. Lin shrugs to herself (🤷): she saw Kev go home, and it is not her business.
  - If you are still in his office when he gives up (crouched behind his desk, say), he points you out of the door
    (👉 you, 🚪) and waits until you have gone: he never leaves anyone in there.
  - About 3:27: he sighs and thinks of coffee (😮‍💨☕). He gives up on Kev and walks out to the kitchen, the length
    of the floor, his screen still black. He is out of sight of his office from about 3:30 and waits at the
    coffee machine until 3:37, then walks back the long way; he is in sight of his office from about 3:41 and at
    his desk about 3:43.
  - 3:42 (or as soon as he is back at his desk): he opens the attachment one last time and counts the faces. The
    sheet pops out big beside his screen. Your face still on it: a tick (📋, your face, ✔️). Your face gone: one
    short, a raised eyebrow, and it is Friday, so he shrugs (📋🤨🤷). Then he packs up for the weekend: coat on
    (🧥), the email to HR open (📧). If his screen is still dead (you pulled the plug and never put it back), he
    sends the same email from his mobile (🧥📱📧); the screen stays black.
  - 3:45: he clicks send. The attachment pops out big, with three faces or four, and flies off his screen.
- **Kev**: young, spiky hair, olive hoodie. The office's computer person. His desk is behind Lin's, by the
  entrance, and his desk phone is on it.
  - 3:01: his phone rings. He picks up, walks into the boss's office, and kneels behind the boss's desk (🔌)
    from 3:05. About 3:08 the screen works, and he goes back to his desk.
  - About 3:15: while the boss is in the restroom, Kev picks up his bag and goes home. He stops beside Lin's
    desk and says goodbye (👋, 🏠). She waves, and turns in her chair to watch him walk out. He leaves by the
    elevator. The boss does not see him go.
- **Lin**: dark hair, teal top. Her desk faces the boss's glass office, and her view cone covers all of it.
  - She is at her desk all day.
  - Her mobile phone lies on her desk. Now and then it buzzes: it shakes on the desk, with buzz lines. She picks
    it up, turns away from the office to read it, and puts it back after about 4 seconds. The buzzes come at
    uneven times (3:04, 3:11.5, 3:17, 3:21, 3:24.5, 3:30.2, 3:33.6, 3:37.5, 3:40, 3:43), so the player
    watches her desk, not a count. While she reads, a thought shows her phone (📱).
  - When a phone rings behind her (Kev's desk), she turns round to it, sees the empty desk, and turns back after
    about 4 seconds, even if it is still ringing. When it is your call, her thought shows where she looks (👀☎️).
  - About 3:08, when the list comes up, she reads it from her desk, through the glass and over the boss's
    shoulder, as she reads everything on that screen: his screen faces her, and she was already watching it. She is frightened (😨), then
    relieved (😌): her face is not on it. Then, once Kev is back past her, she turns her head and looks at you
    for a moment (about 3:10).
  - If she sees you cross your face out, she shouts and points at you (❗👉 you). Then she thinks of the list with
    her own face in your place (📋, her face, 😨): a list with a gap gets filled, and she is the one sitting next
    to it. She gets up and
    goes to tell the boss. Where she meets him, at the latest from the kitchen door, she points you out (👉 you,
    🖥️).

## Ways to win

### Why changing the list saves you
- The card says it: at 3:45 the boss emails HR the layoff list, and you are on it.
- About 3:08 the list pops out large beside his screen: a sheet with a red title bar and four faces, yours in a
  red frame that throbs. You shudder (😨). Then it shrinks back onto his screen, readable from your desk.
- While you take your face off, the sheet pops out large again and stays out: a red line crosses out your face,
  big enough to read from anywhere on the floor. When it is done, your face falls out of the row. Three faces
  remain. Then the sheet closes, saved, and his screen shows the email to HR again, the file attached.
- Before he sends, he counts the faces once more (the sheet pops out big): four, your face ticked; or three, a
  frown and a shrug (📋🤨🤷). That shrug is your edit landing.
- At 3:45 he clicks send: he sends the saved file, which pops out big as it flies off his screen. The win card shows that sheet without your
  face. The lose cards show it with your face framed in red.

### The way in: you pull his plug, he goes for coffee, you plug it back in
Steps, the same for both routes:
1. 3:00 to 3:08: his screen is dead because the plug behind his desk is out, sparking. Kev kneels there, pushes it
   home, and the list comes up. At 3:10 the boss locks the screen and goes to the restroom.
2. While he is away, go behind his desk and "pull his plug out" (1 second). It hangs out of the socket again,
   sparking, and his monitor goes black. Go back to your desk before he walks back. While he sits at his desk,
   you can't get at the plug: he sends you back to work.
3. About 3:22 he sits down to a black screen. He types his password into it blind (✱✱✱✱✱ ⏎), stares (🖥️❓), thumps
   it (😠), phones Kev's
   desk, and nobody comes. About 3:27 he sighs, thinks of coffee (😮‍💨☕) and walks out to the kitchen.
4. When he is out of sight (about 3:30), go back behind his desk and "plug his cable back in" (1.5 seconds). The
   screen blinks on straight onto his open desktop with the list, unlocked: the asterisks you saw went into the PC
   behind the dark monitor, and when he went for coffee there was nothing to lock.
5. Now you must take your face off without Lin seeing you do it. The two routes are the two ways to do this.
6. Be finished before he walks back with his coffee (he leaves the kitchen at 3:37 and is in sight of his office
   from about 3:41). If he sees you taking your face off, you are caught. If he finds you standing around in his
   office, he sends you back to your desk.

In a real office: a boss whose IT man has gone home gives up waiting over a dead screen and gets the coffee he
wanted anyway. He does not lock a screen that shows nothing. He never leaves anyone at his computer: you go in
after he has gone. And the joke is yours: you broke it, he goes for coffee because of you.

### Route 1: wait for Lin's phone (timing)
In his chair, watch Lin's desk. When her phone buzzes and she picks it up and turns away, "take your face off the
list" (3 seconds). She reads for about 4 seconds (📱 over her head). If you start late, she turns back and sees you.

In a real office: a coworker who reads her phone when it buzzes is not watching the boss's office for those
seconds.

### Route 2: ring Kev's phone (a distraction)
His desk phone is next to his screen. "Call Kev's desk." Behind Lin, the empty desk's phone rings. She turns round
to it for about 4 seconds (👀☎️), sees nobody at the empty desk, and turns back. Take your face off while she looks.
This is the boss's own call from 3:01 and 3:24, made by you: the same ring, the same desk, and Lin turns the same way.

In a real office: a phone that rings at an empty desk behind you makes you turn round and look, the same as it did
twice today when the boss called it. You look long enough to see nobody is there.

## Why a real boss would fall for it
- **The way in.** A real boss doesn't leave an employee alone with the layoff list, and he doesn't. His IT person
  went home, his screen is dead (you saw to that), and he goes for a coffee while he waits it out. He locks a
  working screen; a dead one shows nothing, so he leaves it, logged in. Nobody gave you anything: you broke it
  behind his back and mend it behind his back.
- **Route 1.** No one is fooled. Lin checks her phone when it buzzes, like everyone, and you use the moment she
  looks down. The boss is in the kitchen.
- **Route 2.** No one is fooled. A phone ringing at an empty desk behind you makes you turn round. That is what
  people do. The call is from the boss's phone to a desk on the same floor, exactly what the boss did twice today.
- **The send.** He wrote the list at lunch and the email to HR is already open with it attached. At 3:42 on a
  Friday he counts the faces once more; one short, he frowns and shrugs: it is 3:42 on a Friday, and a short list
  is a cheaper list. Then he puts his coat on, clicks send and goes. In a real office: a boss on his way out for the weekend sends the email he
  already wrote.

## Why Lin tells
Lin read the list over the boss's shoulder at 3:08. She was afraid she was on it (😨), and she was not (😌). She
looked at you: she felt for you, while it was you or nobody. When she sees your face go off the list, it is you or
her. The list had four faces; a list with a gap gets a new face, and she sits right in front of it. On screen, she
thinks it before she acts: the list, her own face, 😨. Then she gets up.
It bursts out of her first: ❗👉 you, across the floor. Then she thinks, and goes and tells the boss where she
meets him. As a real person she would think "if he's off it, I'm on it, and I watched him do it," and say
"Um, someone's changing your list." She can tell editing from testing because the screen faces the floor: when you
edit, the sheet comes up big and a red line goes through your face, in plain view of her desk.

## Ways to lose
- **Do nothing.** He works at his desk until 3:42, counts the faces, ticks yours (📋, your face, ✔️), puts his
  coat on (🧥📧). If you pulled the plug and never put it back, he sends it from his mobile (🧥📱📧). At 3:45 the list goes with your face on it. Card: the sheet with your face in a red frame flies off, and you walk out with your desk in a
  box. In a real office: nothing changed, so the list goes as he wrote it.
- **Seen by Lin.** She sees you take your face off, shouts (❗👉 you) and thinks of the list with her own face in your place (📋,
  her face, 😨), gets up and walks toward the kitchen; where she meets the boss, at the latest from the kitchen
  door, she points you out (👉 you, 🖥️). He leaves the coffee,
  walks back to his screen and looks (😠): your face pops back onto the list, and he locks the screen (🔒). He
  stays at his desk and glares out at you (👉 you 😠). At 3:45 he sends the list with your face on it, and you
  are out. Card: Lin at his desk, pointing you out, while he sends it with your face back on it, and you walk out
  with your box (Lin's face, 👉, 📦). In a real office: a coworker who has just escaped the list and sees the gap
  it would leave tells the boss, to keep her own name off it. A boss told that someone is editing his layoff list
  comes back, looks, puts it right, locks it, and sends it. He knows who did it; he does not need to chase you.
- **Caught by the boss.** He walks back with his coffee while you are taking your face off. ❗😠, his red line on
  you, and the screen shakes. Card: the boss on his feet, pointing the way out. In a real office: a boss who finds
  you crossing yourself off his list fires you on the spot.

## The win card
The boss at his desk, pleased, sends the list without you. You sit at your desk in your red sweater, smug. Behind
Lin, Kev's empty desk.

## Who does what
**The boss** comes back from lunch to a dead screen and a list due at 3:45. He bangs on it, which never works, and
phones his IT man, who sits a few desks away. Kev comes and plugs the loose cable back in while the boss stands
over him. Once the list is up he is happy, sits down, and works on it with his back to the floor. At 3:10 he needs
the restroom; he locks his screen as he gets up, because the list is open on it. He walks the long way round the
floor to the restroom and back, and while he is away from his office he never sees Kev leave. If someone pulled
his plug while he was away, he comes back to a dead screen, types his password into it out of habit, phones Kev's
desk, and waits for Kev, who has gone home. Then he gives up and goes for a coffee, leaving the dead screen as it
is. He waits at the machine and walks back. At 3:42 he counts the faces on the list once more, puts his coat on,
and at 3:45 clicks send and goes; if the screen is still dead, he sends it from his mobile. He never fixes the plug himself: he watched Kev do something behind the desk, not what. If Lin tells him you are editing his list, he leaves the coffee, comes back, checks his screen,
puts your face back, locks the screen, and at 3:45 sends it with you on it.

**Kev** is the office's computer person and wants to go home. At 3:01 his desk phone rings; it is the boss. He goes
in, kneels behind the boss's desk, finds the cable loose and plugs it back in. Back at his desk, he waits until
the boss has left his office for the restroom, picks up his bag, says goodbye to Lin and takes the elevator home.
His desk phone stays behind, and so does the boss's habit of calling it.

**Lin** is at her desk all day, facing the boss's office. When her phone buzzes she picks it up and reads it with
her back half turned; when a phone rings behind her, she turns round to it, as anyone does. At 3:08 she reads the
list over the boss's shoulder, finds she is not on it, and looks at you, who are. She waves Kev goodbye. If she
sees you crossing yourself off, she sees her own face filling your gap, and she goes and tells the boss.

## What was cut, and why
The hoodie and the jacket are gone. A boss fooled by clothes contradicts the level's own picture language: people
are faces (the list is a row of faces, and every bubble names people by their faces). Kev keeps his hoodie on all
day. Lin's trip to the restroom is gone, because a watcher who leaves would make both routes a matter of waiting.
The coat rack is gone with the jacket. The boss no longer shows you the list, and he no longer leaves you at his
cable: "fix it, I'm getting coffee" had the most careful man in the office leave the person on his list alone at
his computer. Now he goes for coffee on his own account, and you go in after he has gone. His wave-off (🙅) and
his ☕👉🔌🚪 went with it. So did the boss plugging it in himself when back from coffee: a man who could fix
it in a second would not have gone for coffee, so the coffee trip read as a pretext. The screen no longer dies on a
timer either: a screen that died by itself made the coffee trip something that happened to you. Now you pull the
plug, and everything he does after that is your doing. A dead screen sends from his mobile.

## The joke
You pull the boss's plug while he is in the restroom. He comes back, types his password into a black screen, calls the
man who went home, and gives up and goes to make coffee. You fix it behind his back. Then you pick up his phone and call the desk of
the man who went home, so that the one person watching turns round. While the empty desk rings, you delete
yourself from the layoff list. He comes back, counts one face short, shrugs, and sends it.
