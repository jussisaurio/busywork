# Second Thoughts

## Card

3:00 PM. Your angry resignation letter is in the boss's tray.
He goes through his tray at 3:40.

## Name

"Second Thoughts": you wrote the boss an angry resignation letter, left it in his tray, and now you regret it. The name says what you are in trouble for. It says nothing about how you get out of it.

## The situation

You're an office worker. At lunch you lost your temper, wrote an angry resignation letter and dropped it in the boss's in-tray. It's 3:00 and you've calmed down. You want to keep your job.

The tray is a wire basket on his desk in his glass office. The office has two doors: one onto the hallway, and a side door into the meeting room, right beside the tray. Your white envelope lies in it, and you can see it there from the start. At 3:40 he goes through his tray, as he does every day. If the letter is still there, he reads it, and you're packing a cardboard box.

If you take it back first, there's nothing for him to read. He never knew it existed, so nobody can miss it. Where you are at 3:40 doesn't matter, as long as he hasn't seen you with it.

The trouble is that he sits facing his tray all afternoon, and in the light nothing on his desk escapes him. Taking the letter takes 2.5 seconds, longer than any glance. You need him away from his desk, or blind. The level's one rule is that the dark shrinks every view to arm's length, except along the beam of a phone torch.

## Cast

- **The boss** sits at his desk facing his tray, from 3:00 until 3:40. He never gets up on his own, and he does no work: he dreads the tray. On a fixed five-minute round from 3:01, the same every loop, he looks at it and sighs (📥😮‍💨, a shrug), drums his fingers on the desk, and checks the brass alarm clock at the end of his desk (⏰😮‍💨, a glint off its face). None of it moves his eyes off the desk.
  - 3:39: the desk clock rings. He sighs and drags the tray over.
  - If his office lights go out (their switch is on the lighting panel out in the hallway), he says ❓ and holds up his phone. He gets up, goes out through the side door and the meeting room into the hallway, and walks to the panel. Its switches have no labels: he tries one after another (his office tubes blink as he does) for about two minutes until his own come back on, then walks back. His desk is empty for about ten minutes. Whoever he finds standing at the panel when he gets there switched it off.
  - If the whole floor goes dark (the fuse), he stays put and holds up his phone as a torch, pointed at his hallway door (waiting for someone to come and tell him what happened). The beam lights the front of his desk and that door, the same every loop. The side of his desk and the side door stay dark.
  - If you throw up on the floor (see "Eating it"), he hears the retching from his desk. He gets up and walks over to see what it was (🤢). If your envelope is lying in the sick, he knows his own envelope: ✉️😠, and he points at you.
  - 3:40: he goes through his tray, there at his desk. If your letter is in it, he reads it: ✉️😠.
- **Lin** sits at her desk on the open floor.
  - 3:05: she goes to the kitchen for her afternoon coffee and switches on the old coffee machine at about 3:10. Left alone, the old machine only makes the lights flicker all over the floor: she's startled, Kev looks down the hall at the fuse box, the fuse holds and the lights steady. Three minutes later it has brewed: green lamp, full pot. A full pot pours a cup, for you too; the cup leads nowhere.
  - If someone poured the water jug down the back of the machine onto its wall socket first, it shorts when she switches it on: sparks and smoke, and a spark runs down the wiring to the fuse box and trips it for the whole floor. She sees nothing wrong before she switches it on: the water is behind the machine, and the jug stands back in its place, empty. A shorted machine brews nothing. If the power is already out, nothing happens and she's puzzled.
  - If you throw up on the floor, she says 🤢 and goes to the kitchen for paper towels. Once the boss has had his look, she wipes it all up, envelope and all, and bins it. She never touches the envelope by itself.
  - 3:23: she pours herself a cup if it brewed, and drinks it at the kitchen table. 3:31: back at her desk, her cup beside her.
- **Kev** sits at the far desk on the open floor. He's the one who resets fuses. From his chair he sees through the kitchen door to the front of the coffee machine.
  - Whenever the power goes out, he holds up his phone and walks down the hallway to the fuse box at the far west end. The breakers have no labels, so he finds the tripped one by phone light, which takes him a few minutes, and the lights come back on.
  - If the coffee machine shorted and is smoking, he goes to the kitchen first and unplugs it (❓), and only then walks to the fuse box: he won't reset a fuse with a smoking machine still plugged in.
  - If he finds someone standing at the fuse box, he takes them for the one who pulled it and tells the boss. You're sent to your desk to do assigned work for 20 minutes.

## Ways to win

Every route is the same last step: take the letter from the tray while the boss is away from his desk or blinded, then keep it out of his sight once he's back and the light is on. Walk well away from his glass office (your own desk is far enough), slip it into your desk drawer, or eat it.

Eating it is a red herring. Hold E with the letter in hand (a tap drops it): you chew, wince (😖) and bits of paper fly. Ten minutes later it comes back up. Your face goes green (🤢), then you throw up on the floor behind your chair: a green puddle, the half-chewed envelope lying soggy in it (stained but whole, his name still on it), a retch heard all over the floor, and everyone turns. Nobody picks a letter out of that, you included. Lin goes for paper towels; the boss gets up to see what the retching was. If he reaches the puddle, he knows his envelope in it, and you're caught. If it comes up after about 3:38, he goes through his tray at 3:40 first and finds it empty: a loss card all the same, the envelope lying in the sick in front of him. Eat it after about 3:30 and it is still inside you at 3:40: a win, with its own card.

In a real office: someone who swallows a paper envelope may well bring it back up, and a boss who hears retching comes to look. If his own envelope is in it, he knows it.

### The switch

Steps:

1. Walk out to the lighting panel in the hallway, on the meeting-room wall (pretend to be busy on the way), and switch his office lights off.
2. Walk away from the panel at once: he comes out to it, and whoever he finds there switched it off.
3. While he is out at the panel, go into his office by the hallway door, take the letter from his tray, and get away from his office before he walks back.

In a real office: when the lights in your own office go out, you go and find out why. If the switch is on an unlabelled panel down the hall, you spend a couple of minutes trying switches, and your desk is empty all that time.

Facts, and where you see each one:

- **Your letter is in his tray, and he's watching it.** 3:00, the opening frame: the white envelope lies in the wire basket on his desk, he sits facing it, and his sight cone covers the whole desk.
- **Taking it in the light gets you caught.** Try it: he points at you, and the end card shows him pointing at you from his chair as you reach for his tray.
- **His office lights are on a panel in the hallway.** From 3:00: a white lighting panel on the hallway wall of the meeting room, well away from his office and out of his view. Stand by it and a prompt appears.
- **Only his office goes dark.** The moment you switch it: his glass office turns navy, the rest of the floor stays lit, and the panel shows an amber lamp. From the lit floor, the dark glass is mostly reflections.
- **He goes looking for the cause.** Right after the switch: a puzzled thought over his head and a phone flash in his hand. He gets up, walks out by the side door through the meeting room, and comes down the hallway to the panel. There he tries the switches one by one (his office tubes blink) for about two minutes until his lights come on, then walks back the way he came. His desk is empty for about ten minutes.
- **Don't be at the panel when he gets there.** Whoever he finds standing at it switched it off, pretending or not. The end card shows him pointing at you in the hallway beside the panel.
- **Don't let him see you with it.** If he sees the envelope in your hand once he's back and the light is on, he catches you. The end card shows him pointing at you holding it.

### The blackout

Steps:

1. Before 3:10, walk into the kitchen and pour the water jug down the back of the coffee machine, onto its wall socket. Do it from beside the machine (by the fridge), where Kev can't see you. Pretend to be busy on the way.
2. At about 3:10 Lin switches it on: it shorts, and the whole floor goes dark.
3. Walk to the side of his desk, by the side door, outside his torch beam, and take the letter.
4. Leave by the side door, and get away from his office before Kev turns the power back on.

In a real office: water on a plug socket shorts whatever is plugged into it and trips the fuse. Nobody resets a fuse while the machine that blew it is still smoking, so the floor stays dark for a while. In a blackout a boss sits tight with his phone torch and waits for someone to come and tell him what happened.

Facts, and where you see each one:

- **The old coffee machine strains the wiring.** Any first loop, just by waiting. 3:05: Lin walks to the coffee machine on the kitchen counter. At about 3:10 she switches it on and the lights all over the floor flicker. She's startled, and Kev, at his desk, turns to look down the hall at the fuse box. The lights steady, and the machine brews.
- **A full water jug stands beside it, and you can pour it down the back.** From 3:00: a glass jug of water on the counter beside the machine. Stand by the machine and a prompt appears. Afterwards the jug stands empty in its place, and a dark wet streak shows on the wall behind the machine's shoulder. Nothing shows on its front.
- **Kev watches the front of the machine.** From his desk, his sight cone reaches through the kitchen door to the front of the machine. Pour the jug from there and he sees you, points you out to the boss, and you're sent to your desk to work. From beside the machine, by the fridge, he can't see you.
- **A wet socket shorts it and the whole floor goes dark.** At about 3:10: sparks and a burst of grey smoke, Lin startled, and a spark runs out of the kitchen and down the wiring to the fuse box at the end of the hallway. Its door flies open, a red lamp blinks, and the office, the meeting room, the kitchen and the open floor all go dark. Only the strip of floor by the glass lobby keeps its daylight. The machine stays scorched black, smoking until Kev unplugs it.
- **In the dark, the boss only sees where his phone points.** From the blackout: everyone holds up a phone with its flash lit, and each throws a lit beam on the floor. His beam rests on his hallway door, across the front of his desk. The side of his desk, beside the tray, and the side door stay dark.
- **The dark lasts about thirteen minutes.** Right after it: Kev walks his torch to the kitchen and unplugs the smoking machine, then walks down the hallway to the fuse box and hunts for the tripped breaker by phone light. The lights come back at about 3:24.

### The breaker

Steps:

1. Walk to the fuse box at the west end of the hallway and pull the main breaker. Any time works.
2. Get out of the hallway at once, walk to the side of his desk outside his beam, and take the letter.
3. Get away from his office before the lights come back.

In a real office: pulling the main breaker puts the floor in the dark. Whoever goes to fix it has to walk the length of the floor and find the right switch among unlabelled ones by phone light, which takes minutes, and anyone found standing at the box gets the blame.

Facts, and where you see each one:

- **The fuse box runs the floor's lights.** From 3:00: a grey metal box with a yellow high-voltage sticker on the end wall of the hallway, door shut, green lamp. At 3:10, when the lights flicker, Kev looks straight at it.
- **You can pull it yourself.** Stand at it and a prompt appears. Pulling it puts out every light on the floor. Its door hangs open and its lamp blinks red.
- **The boss can't see the fuse box from his chair.** The opening frame: it's around the corner in the hallway, behind the solid south-west wall of his office, outside his sight cone.
- **Kev comes to the fuse box, so don't be there.** A second or so after the lights go out, Kev gets up at the far end of the floor and walks his torch down the whole hallway to it. If you're still there when he arrives, he tells the boss, and you're sent to your desk to work.
- **The dark lasts about nine minutes.** Kev's walk takes most of it; at the box he hunts among the unlabelled breakers by phone light (his hands in the box) before the lights come back.

## Ways to lose

- **3:40 and the letter is still in his tray** (or he picked it up after you dropped it where he could see it). The end card: he holds your envelope up over his desk, furious, his tray empty, and you stand sweating in front of it; the card's 📦 says the rest.
  In a real office: a boss who finds an angry resignation letter in his tray reads it, and you are out.
- **Caught with it.** You reached for it while he could see you, or he saw it in your hand. The end card: his light on, he points at you, and you stand where he saw you, reaching for the tray or with the envelope in your hand.
  In a real office: a boss who watches you take an envelope out of his own tray knows exactly what you took.
- **Found at the panel.** You switched off his lights and stayed by the panel. The end card: out in the lit hallway he points at you beside the panel, his switch still off.
  In a real office: the person standing at the light switch when the lights went out is the one who switched them off.
- **Found at the fuse box.** Kev arrives and finds you standing at it, and tells the boss. You lose 20 minutes at your desk doing assigned work. That usually means the 3:40 card above.
  In a real office: the person standing at the open fuse box is the one who pulled it.
- **The letter came back up and he saw it.** You ate it too early. He came over to see what the retching was and knew his own envelope in the sick. The end card: he stands over the green puddle on the floor, pointing at the soggy envelope lying in it, furious (✉️😠), and you stand beside it, green.
  In a real office: a boss who hears someone retching comes to look, and he knows an envelope addressed to him when he sees one, soggy or not.
- **Seen soaking the coffee machine.** Kev sees you pour the jug from the front of the machine, and points you out to the boss: 20 minutes of assigned work at your desk, and the machine stays dry.
  In a real office: someone seen pouring water over the office coffee machine gets reported.

## The joke

The blackout win. The room is dark. The boss sits at his desk with his phone torch trained on his door, guarding his tray, and the tray is empty. Just outside his beam you slip out of the side door, smug, with the envelope in your hand. In the kitchen, in Kev's torchlight, Lin's coffee machine is still smoking, unplugged at last, scorched up the wall.

## End cards

Every card shows the day as it ended (3:40 for the 3:40 cards, the moment you were caught otherwise), drawn from the state: what you hold, where each person is, the lights. A win: the lights on, the boss at his desk holding up his empty tray, baffled (❓), and you at your desk (or wherever you are) with whatever you hold. Behind, the route's aftermath: the hallway lighting panel after the switch (his eyes keep going out to it); the coffee machine you soaked, scorched, its jug empty beside it and a wet streak behind it, after the blackout; the fuse box after the breaker.

Other cards: the letter eaten and still inside you at 3:40 (a win: he holds up his empty tray, baffled; you sit at your desk, green, thinking 🤢). Sent to your desk by Kev and still there at 3:40 (the boss at his desk, furious, your letter in his hand; you at your desk behind a pile of assigned paperwork; @kev 👉 📄 ✉️). Caught outside his office: in the hallway, by the panel or the fuse box, whoever caught you points at you (in the dark, with his phone torch on you).

## Who does what

**The boss** comes back from lunch at 3:00 to a tray he does not want to open. He is a manager who hates paperwork, so he puts it off: he sits facing the tray, sighs at it, drums his fingers, and checks the brass clock on his desk every few minutes, hoping it is later than it is. He does no other work. At 3:39 the clock rings, he gives up, drags the tray over and at 3:40 goes through it, as every day. When his office lights go out, he does what anyone does: gets up and goes to the panel in the hallway to see why, and fumbles with its unlabelled switches. When the whole floor goes out, he stays at his desk with his phone pointed at his door, because somebody will come and tell him what happened. When he hears someone retching, he goes over to see. If someone tells him a colleague was messing with the fuse box or the coffee machine, he gives that person a pile of work to keep them at their desk.

**Lin** works at the desk nearest his office. At 3:05 she wants her afternoon coffee (☕), walks to the kitchen and at about 3:10 switches on the old machine that always makes the lights flicker. She waits for it, pours a cup at 3:23, drinks it at the kitchen table and is back at her desk at 3:31. She is helpful and squeamish: if a colleague throws up, she fetches paper towels and wipes it up, and whatever is in it goes in the bin with the towels.

**Kev** works at the far desk. He is the floor's unofficial handyman: when the lights flicker he looks at the fuse box, and when the power goes he takes his phone torch to the fuse box and resets it. If the coffee machine is smoking, he unplugs it first. From his chair he can see through the kitchen door to the front of the coffee machine. He is also the office tattletale: if he finds someone at the fuse box or sees someone pour water on the coffee machine, he points them out to the boss (❗👉).

**You** wrote an angry resignation letter at lunch and put it in his tray. It is 3:00, you have calmed down, and you want it back before he reads it.

## Things in the office

- **His in-tray (wire basket) and your envelope in it.** The goal, in plain view from the first frame.
- **His desk and his brass desk clock.** Why he sits there all afternoon: he keeps checking the clock and at 3:39 it rings. It tells you the deadline without text.
- **His glass office walls.** You can see the tray, and him, from the open floor. He can see out, too. Dark inside a lit floor, the glass mostly shows reflections.
- **The lighting panel** (hallway wall of the meeting room, out of his view). A row of unlabelled switches; one is his office lights. The switch route: his office goes dark, and only his, and he has to come out to the panel to fix it.
- **His hallway door and the side door to the meeting room.** The hallway door is where his torch points in a blackout; the side door, beside the tray, is the dark way in and out, and the way he walks out to the panel.
- **The fuse box** (west end of the hallway, round the corner from his office). Unlabelled breakers. The breaker route, and where Kev goes in every blackout.
- **The old coffee machine** (kitchen counter), plugged into a wall socket behind it. It strains the wiring at 3:10 (the flicker), so a short in it plausibly trips the floor. Brewed, its pot pours a cup that leads nowhere.
- **The glass water jug beside it.** Pour it down the back of the machine onto the socket, and the 3:10 switch-on shorts it: the blackout route. Nothing shows from the front.
- **The fridge.** Where you stand to pour the jug out of Kev's view.
- **The kitchen paper towels** (on the kitchen counter). What Lin fetches if you throw up.
- **The kitchen table.** Where Lin drinks her coffee from 3:23 to 3:31, away from her desk.
- **Your desk, with its drawer.** Where you sit; where Kev's assigned work lands; and a drawer you can hide the letter in, out of the boss's sight.
- **Lin's and Kev's desks.** Where they sit when nothing is happening; Kev's has the view into the kitchen.
- **Everyone's phone.** In the dark, each person lights only what their phone torch points at. This is the level's one rule.
- **The glass lobby, the elevator and the restroom.** The rest of a real office floor. The lobby's daylight keeps one strip of floor lit in a blackout.
- **The puddle** (only if you eat the letter). Where it came back up, the soggy envelope in it; what brings the boss over.
