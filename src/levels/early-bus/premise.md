# The Last Bus

## Card
3:00 PM. The last bus leaves at 4:30. The boss leaves at 4:50.
Nobody leaves before him.

## Name
"The Last Bus": your bus leaves at 4:30, twenty minutes before the boss does, and nobody leaves before him. The name and
the card say what you want and why it is a problem. They say nothing about how you get out of it.

## The situation
You work in an open-plan office. The last bus home stops outside the lobby's glass door at 4:30. The boss goes home at
4:50 sharp, every day, and the office rule is that nobody leaves before him. His coat hangs on a rack in the hallway,
by his office door: while it hangs there, he is in. If nothing changes, at 4:30 the boss is still at his desk, so you
stay at yours and watch your bus drive away. If he has gone home by 4:30, nothing keeps you, and you get on the bus if
you are at the stop. The day is a time loop: when you lose, it is 3:00 again.

## The rules
- **Everyone lives by the clock of the room he is in.** There are two wall clocks: the boss's clock in his office
  (behind his chair) and the open-plan clock on the hallway wall (over Kev's desk, on the boss's way back from the
  kitchen). A person looks up at the clock of the room he stands in and thinks of its time (a clock face). Whoever
  reads a clock stands near it. Every time below is what the person believes.
- **Nobody believes a clock that jumped under his nose.** Lin and Kev look at the open-plan clock all afternoon. If it
  says something different from what they saw last, they frown at it (the time, 🤨) and keep their own time.
- **The boss believes the first clock he sees after lunch.** He is out at a long lunch from before 3:00, and his phone
  is flat (📱🪫), so he has not seen the time for an hour. Back at about 3:45, he fetches a coffee and walks back under
  the open-plan clock. He believes whatever it says (if it is later than he thought: the time, 😮). Then he sits at his
  desk and turns round to his own clock. If the two agree (within ten minutes), he believes it. If they do not, he
  frowns at it (the time, 🤨) and climbs up to set the clocks right (the time, 😠).
- **He has seen his own clock tick.** Once he is at his desk, his clock cannot jump without him knowing. If it moves
  while you are in his office, he looks from the clock to you (your face, 🤨) and sets it right.
- **Nobody leaves before the boss.** He takes his coat only when he goes home. At 3:13 Kev picks up his laptop to go,
  looks at the coat on the rack (🧥😬) and puts the laptop down again.
- **He checks your desk.** After each look at his clock the boss looks out through the glass at your desk. You there:
  your face, ✔️. Your chair empty: your face, ❓, and he comes looking for you: his office, the open plan, the lobby,
  the bus stop. If you are already out of the door, he rings you first (📱, your face, ❓; your phone buzzes, 📳). If he
  finds you inside on the phone, he lets it go. If he sees you out in the street while he is still at work, you are
  caught.

## Cast
- **The caretaker** (blue work jacket). At 3:00 he is up the stepladder under the boss's clock, changing its battery
  (🔋). Soon after he has gone, and he leaves both stepladders where they stand.
- **The boss** (navy suit, shirtsleeves until he goes home; glass office top left). He sits with his back to his clock,
  facing the glass and the open plan.
  - 3:00 to about 3:45: out at a long lunch. He comes back along the street, past the bus stop and in through the
    lobby, thinking of his flat phone (📱🪫).
  - He pours a coffee in the kitchen (about 3:49) and walks back under the open-plan clock (about 3:54) and reads it.
    If it says 4:50 or later, he takes his coat from the rack in the hallway and goes home from there.
  - Otherwise he sits down at about 3:57, turns round to his clock and checks it against the open-plan clock (see the
    rules), then looks at your desk.
  - He turns round to his clock again at 4:10, 4:25 and 4:28, each time followed by a look at your desk.
  - 4:50: he takes his coat off the rack, waves, and goes home through the lobby and over the zebra crossing.
  - Told about a stepladder, he sends you to your desk with 20 minutes of work and sets the pushed clocks right.
- **Lin** (teal cardigan, the desk below the boss's glass wall, a few steps from the open-plan clock). She sits facing
  the glass and sees the boss's stepladder through it. Anyone who climbs it gets told on.
  - She looks up at the open-plan clock at 3:12, 3:50, 4:10 and 4:25.
  - 3:20: she goes to the restroom (🚻, bottom right). She is out of sight until about 3:35.
- **Kev** (olive hoodie, the desk right under the open-plan clock, facing it). Anyone on that stepladder is in front of
  his eyes.
  - 3:01 to about 3:07: a coffee from the pot in the kitchen (top right). He goes again at 3:25, back at about 3:31.
  - He looks up at the open-plan clock when he sits down, and at 3:20, 3:55, 4:10 and 4:25.
  - 3:13: the laptop and the coat (see the rules).
- **You** (red jumper, the middle desk). You can climb the stepladder under either clock and push the clock 10 minutes
  forward. Each push takes a moment, moves the hands on 10 minutes, paints the extra time red on the face and makes
  the clock hang a little more crooked. Walking about you take a phone call ('pretend'); at your desk you type.
  - If the boss turns round to his clock while you are on its stepladder, you are caught.
  - If Lin or Kev sees you on a stepladder, they shout and point at you (!, finger, you). If the boss is in, he sends
    you back to your desk with 20 minutes of work and the push you were doing does not count. If he is out, they tell
    him when he walks back in (finger, clock), and he sets the clocks right.

## Who does what
The caretaker is there before you can do anything. At 3:00 he stands on the stepladder under the boss's clock and
puts a new battery in it. He did the open-plan clock first. He leaves both stepladders where they stand and goes out
through the lobby. He does not care what anyone else does.

The boss is out at a long lunch when the day starts, and his phone is flat. At about 3:45 he walks back along the
street, past the bus stop and in through the lobby. He goes to the kitchen and pours a coffee. On his way back he walks
under the open-plan clock and reads it. It is the first clock he has seen since lunch, so he believes it. If it says
home time, he takes his coat from the rack in the hallway and goes home. If not, he sits down in his glass office with
his back to his clock, turns round to it, and compares it with the open-plan clock. If they agree, he believes his
clock. If not, he sets both clocks right. Then he looks out at your desk. He does that again at 4:10, 4:25 and 4:28.
If your chair is empty, he rings you if you are out, and gets up to look for you. At 4:50 by the clock in front of
him, he takes his coat off the rack in the hallway, waves and goes home over the zebra crossing.

Lin works at the desk below the boss's glass wall. She faces it, so she sees his stepladder through the glass. She
looks up at the open-plan clock every quarter of an hour or so. If it has jumped, she frowns and keeps her own time.
At 3:20 she goes to the restroom and comes back at about 3:35. If she sees someone on a stepladder, she shouts and
points. She tells the boss at once if he is in, or when he walks back in.

Kev works at the desk right under the open-plan clock, facing it. At 3:01 he goes to the kitchen for a coffee and comes
back at about 3:07. If the clock over his desk has jumped while he was away, he frowns at it and keeps his own time. At
3:13 he picks up his laptop to go home, sees the boss's coat on the rack, puts the laptop down and sits down again. At
3:25 he goes for another coffee and comes back at about 3:31. Like Lin, he tells on anyone he sees on a stepladder.

## Things in the office
- **The boss's wall clock**, in his office behind his chair. He keeps the time by it once he is at his desk, but only
  if it agrees with the open-plan clock he passed on his way in. Route 1 needs it pushed.
- **The open-plan clock**, on the hallway wall over Kev's desk and on the boss's way back from the kitchen. Lin and Kev
  look at it all afternoon. It is the first clock the boss sees after lunch. Both routes need it pushed.
- **Two stepladders**, one under each clock. The caretaker leaves them there after the battery change. They are how
  you reach a clock, and anyone on one is easy to see.
- **The coat rack and the boss's coat**, in the hallway by his office door. The coat on the rack means he is in. It
  shows the rule (Kev at 3:13) and shows when he has gone. Because it hangs in the hallway, the boss can go home from
  there without going back into his office.
- **The boss's flat phone.** It is why the wall clocks are all he has after lunch.
- **The glass wall** of the boss's office. He sees your desk through it, and Lin sees his stepladder through it.
- **The desks**: the boss's desk, yours, Lin's and Kev's. Your empty chair is what makes him look for you. The empty
  desks at the sides make the office feel real.
- **The kitchen** with the coffee pot, the fridge and a table. Kev goes there at 3:01, which is your time on the
  open-plan clock. The boss goes there when he is back from lunch, which takes him under that clock.
- **The restroom.** Lin goes there at 3:20, which is your time on the boss's clock.
- **The plants.** They make the office feel real and do nothing else.
- **The lobby, the glass door, the bus stop sign, the road and the zebra crossing.** The bus stops at the sign at 4:30.
  The boss comes back from lunch past it and goes home over the crossing, so he sees anyone waiting at the stop
  before he has gone.
- **The bus.** It pulls in at about 4:26 and leaves at 4:30.

## Ways to win
You win when the boss has gone home before 4:30 and you are at the bus stop at 4:30. The bus pulls in at 4:26 and you
get on. You see the stop at the start (a bus stop sign by the road, outside the lobby's glass door) and the bus at the
kerb (shot `bus-arrives`).

### 1. Push both clocks half an hour (route icon: clock)
Steps:
1. At 3:01, when Kev gets up for his coffee, climb the stepladder over his desk and push the open-plan clock three
   times (+30). Get down before he is back at about 3:07.
2. At 3:20, when Lin goes to the restroom, leave your desk on the phone, walk into the boss's empty office, climb the
   stepladder behind his chair and push his clock three times (+30). Get out before Lin is back at about 3:35.
3. Be at your desk when he sits down at about 3:57.
4. Walking back with his coffee he reads the open-plan clock: half an hour later than he thought (😮); lunch ran long.
   At his desk his own clock agrees, so he believes it, and sees you at your desk (✔️). From now on he lives half an
   hour early: he takes his coat at about 4:22 and walks out through the lobby; he is over the crossing by about 4:28.
5. Follow him out, behind him, and wait at the stop. It is close: you reach it just before 4:30.

In a real office: a boss back from a long lunch with a flat phone looks at the hallway clock and then at his own. If
both say the same, he believes them, and he goes home half an hour early. If only one is wrong, he notices. If he sees
you next to his clock as it jumps, he knows what you did.

The facts, and when you see them:
- **The clocks are set by somebody else.** At 3:00 the caretaker is up the boss's stepladder with a battery (shot
  `start`); he leaves the stepladders behind.
- **People here go by the wall clock.** Kev looks up at the open-plan clock (shot `kev-checks-the-clock`), and so does
  Lin (shot `lin-checks-the-open-clock`).
- **You can push a clock forward.** Up close each is a real wall clock with a stepladder under it (shots
  `boss-clock-close`, `open-clock-close`). After you push it, the hands read later than the HUD clock, the extra time
  is red and it hangs crooked (shots `open-clock-pushed`, `boss-clock-pushed`).
- **Kev and Lin are away for a while.** Kev pours a coffee at 3:01 (shot `kev-pours-a-coffee`); Lin walks off to the
  restroom at 3:20 (shot `lin-goes-to-the-restroom`).
- **Lin and Kev do not believe a jump.** Kev comes back and frowns at the pushed clock (shot `kev-doubts`). Nothing
  they do depends on it.
- **Lin watches his stepladder.** Her vision cone reaches through the glass to it (shot `start`). Try while she is at
  her desk and she points and shouts (shot `lin-tells`); she tells the boss when he walks in, and he sets the clocks
  right (shot `lin-tells-the-boss`).
- **The boss has no other clock.** He walks in with a flat phone (shot `boss-back-from-lunch`) and pours a coffee
  (shot `boss-pours-a-coffee`).
- **He reads the open-plan clock first.** He looks up at it on his way back (shot `boss-reads-the-open-clock`); with
  it pushed, he is surprised (shot `boss-reads-both`).
- **Then his own clock, against it.** It agrees (shot `boss-checks-his-clock`), and he looks at your desk (shot
  `boss-glances-at-your-desk`). Push only his clock and he frowns at it (shot `boss-frowns-at-his-clock`) and sets it
  right (shot `boss-sets-it-right`). Stay in his office and he looks at you (shot `boss-suspects`). Be on the
  stepladder when he walks in and he catches you there (shot `end-caught`).
- **His coat means he is in.** At 3:13 Kev looks at it and stays (shot `kev-sees-the-coat`); the boss puts it on when
  he goes (shot `boss-takes-his-coat`).
- **+30 is enough and +20 is not.** The card gives the sums: he leaves at 4:50 by his clock and your bus goes at 4:30,
  and he needs a few minutes to walk out. With +20 he is still at his desk at 4:30.
- **Don't go before him.** Leave your desk while he is in and he finds your chair empty, rings you and comes looking
  (shots `boss-hunts`, `boss-rings-you`); out in the street, he catches you (shot `end-caught-in-the-street`).

### 2. Push the open-plan clock an hour (route icon: coffee)
Steps:
1. At 3:01, when Kev gets up for his coffee, climb the stepladder over his desk.
2. Push the open-plan clock six times (+60). Get down before Kev is back at about 3:07.
3. Go back to your desk.

Kev sits down, looks up and frowns (🤨): it said 3:01 a moment ago. Lin frowns at it at 3:12. They keep their own time.
At about 3:54 the boss, back from lunch with his coffee, walks under the open-plan clock: it says 4:54, and he believes
it (😮). He takes his coat off the rack in the hallway at about 3:56 and goes home without going back into his office,
so he never sees his own clock. He is gone by about 4:02. Then go out to the stop.

In a real office: a boss back from a long lunch with a flat phone sees home time on the hallway clock with a coffee in
his hand. His coat is right there, so he takes it and goes.

The facts, and when you see them:
- **Kev is away at 3:01.** You see him walk off to the kitchen and pour a coffee (shots `open-clock-close`,
  `kev-pours-a-coffee`).
- **Lin and Kev do not believe a jump.** Kev frowns at the pushed clock (shot `kev-doubts`).
- **The boss reads the open-plan clock when he walks back from the kitchen.** He has a flat phone (shot
  `boss-back-from-lunch`), pours a coffee (shot `boss-pours-a-coffee`) and looks up at it (shot
  `boss-reads-the-open-clock`).
- **At 4:50 or later he goes.** He believes the clock (shot `boss-reads-home-time`) and takes his coat from the
  hallway rack (shot `boss-takes-his-coat-early`).
- **+60 is enough and +50 is not.** +50 shows 4:44, so he walks on to his desk, where his own clock disagrees.
- **Don't go before him.** Go out before he has taken his coat and he catches you in the street (shot
  `end-caught-in-the-street`).

## Ways to lose
- **The boss is still in at 4:30, and you are at your desk.** The end card: 4:30 on the boss's clock; he is at his desk
  in his shirtsleeves with his coat on the rack, so you stay; out of the window your bus drives off (shot `end-fail`).
- **The boss is still in at 4:30, and you are at the stop.** He rang you (📳), and he fetches you back to your desk.
  The end card: you at your desk, the boss pointing at you, his coat glowing on its rack, and out of the window the
  bus pulls away (shot `end-fail-at-the-stop`).
- **Caught on the stepladder.** He turns round to his clock, or walks in, while you are up it. The end card: you up
  the stepladder at his clock, and the boss below pointing up at you, angry (shot `end-caught`).
- **Caught in the street.** Out at the stop while he is still at work: he passes it on his way back from lunch, or
  comes looking for you after seeing your empty chair. The end card, in daylight with no bus: you at the stop, the
  boss pointing at you from the lobby door, his coat glowing on its rack through the window (shot
  `end-caught-in-the-street`).
- **The boss went home early, but you were not at the stop at 4:30.** The end card: you at your desk in the empty
  office, the coat rack empty, and out of the window the bus pulls away (shot `end-blamed`).
- **Told on, or found out.** Not a loss by itself. Lin or Kev points at you, or the boss sees that his clock disagrees
  or moved. He sets the clocks right, and usually the window has closed.

## The joke
3:54 on the open-plan route. The boss, whose rule keeps everyone at their desks, comes back from a long lunch with a
flat phone and a fresh coffee, glances at the hallway clock, believes it, and grabs his coat off the rack in a hurry.
Kev and Lin, who frowned at that clock an hour ago, stay at their desks and watch the man who makes the rule leave at
four.
